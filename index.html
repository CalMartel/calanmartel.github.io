VIEW WORK GET IN TOUCH ABOUT I'm a materials engineer focused on electrochemical energy storage — the science of how we capture, hold, and release energy at scale. I grew up in the Sierra Nevada foothills, and the mountains shaped how I think: carefully, with respect for what's already there. I earned my MEng at UC Berkeley's Fung Institute, concentrating in Electrochemical Materials Engineering. Before that, I spent two years as a battery engineer at NexTech Batteries, working across the full lifecycle of early-stage battery development — from wet lab electrochemistry and cell assembly to process engineering, data analysis, and market strategy. I'm about to join Green Grid Energy Storage to work on liquid salt battery systems for the grid — a technology I believe has real potential to change how we store renewable energy at scale. I'm also currently taking an electrochemical battery device course through UC Berkeley to deepen the technical foundation I'll need for this work. Key contributions: ELECTROCHEMISTRY & CELL DEVELOPMENT Designed and ran structured DOE experiments on Li-S pouch cells, including temperature-dependent cycling studies. Assembled custom Arduino-based thermoelectric sensing devices — handling all soldering and hardware assembly — integrated directly into cell loading fixtures to enable continuous temperature monitoring during cycling. Researched and proposed MXene (titanium carbide nanosheets) as a potential cathode additive to reduce polysulfide shuttling and extend cycle life. Identified Drexel University as the leading source and independently researched an in-house synthesis pathway. The direction was well-received but deprioritized as the team shifted focus to other projects. Proposed and helped direct a post-mortem analysis (PMA) research initiative, examining Li-S cells at multiple states of health via SEM. The imaging was conducted by the team's PhD chemist at the University of Nevada, Reno, based on the research direction I developed. Developed a standardized capacity-vs-cycle analysis framework using Q1/Q2/Q3 discharge curve segmentation, enabling systematic benchmarking of new cell chemistries against a consistent baseline. MANUFACTURING & PROCESS ENGINEERING Diagnosed and resolved a chronic electrolyte leakage failure caused by unequal heat distribution during pouch sealing — identified the root cause (mismatched sealing requirements between tab-to-pouch and pouch-to-pouch regions) and proposed a selective heating solution. Helped design and build an electrolyte vacuum fill system using an Edwards vacuum pump and sealed syringe setup, eliminating trapped air and separator wrinkling and improving cell consistency across batches. Designed and 3D printed multiple custom production components: battery jig plates and stands, pressure-distribution test fixtures, slurry homogenization cups, spooler guide blocks, and vacuum fan blades — the latter restoring house vacuum efficiency from 30% back to 100%. Applied Hooke's Law and Fujifilm Prescale witness paper to verify 30 psi cell test pressure across eight springs, confirming spacer dimensions were correct and informing a complete redesign of the final production test jig. Researched and sourced separator materials, identifying that SK separator failures were driven by lower puncture strength relative to available alternatives. Developed and documented the company's lithium metal anode QC procedure and cutting protocol, and helped establish ECN (Engineering Change Notice) documentation standards. STRATEGY & DATA Helped implement and maintain the company's internal DOE tracking database — organizing experiment purpose, goals, status, priority, and outcomes in a structured system used across the engineering team. Took initiative to approach company leadership directly with a proposal to contribute to business strategy. Benchmarked company cell specs against LG Chem, Samsung, and CEC grid storage cells to help establish a competitive baseline for pouch cell performance.
